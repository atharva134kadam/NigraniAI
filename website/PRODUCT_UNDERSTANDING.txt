# 1. Introduction – What is NigraniAI?

NigraniAI is a privacy and compliance monitoring platform designed to help organisations understand, monitor, and manage how personal data is processed across their technical environments.

Organisations collect and process personal information through websites, applications, APIs, databases, and internal services. However, they may not always have complete visibility into:

- What personal data is being collected.
- Where personal data is stored.
- Which applications and services access it.
- Why personal data is being processed.
- Whether processing is aligned with consent and declared purposes.
- Whether data is being retained beyond applicable deadlines.
- Whether suspicious activities or potential data breaches are occurring.
- Whether sufficient compliance evidence is available.

NigraniAI addresses these challenges by analysing actual system activity, available customer database information, consent records, retention schedules, and configured privacy policies.

It brings multiple privacy-monitoring capabilities together in one platform.

**In simple terms:**

NigraniAI continuously monitors how an organisation handles personal data, identifies potential privacy and compliance issues, helps investigate suspicious activities, and provides information that supports corrective action and compliance readiness.

It is not merely a compliance checklist or a document-management tool. It is designed around actual system activity and data-processing information.

# 2. The Problem NigraniAI Solves

Traditional compliance approaches often depend on manual assessments, questionnaires, spreadsheets, and periodic reviews.

These approaches may not reveal what is actually happening inside an organisation's applications and databases.

For example:

1. An application may process customer information without a corresponding valid consent record.
2. A service may use personal data for a purpose that has not been declared.
3. Customer information may remain stored beyond its applicable retention period.
4. Personal data may appear in application logs.
5. A service may suddenly access an unusually large number of customer records.
6. Sensitive database tables may be exposed through public access.
7. An organisation may struggle to identify the evidence supporting a compliance finding.

NigraniAI aims to provide continuous visibility into these activities instead of depending only on manual compliance reviews.

# 3. How NigraniAI Works – Overall Workflow

The platform follows a connected privacy-monitoring workflow.

## Step 1: Connect the Environment

The system connects to available organisational data sources, such as:

- Application services.
- API activity.
- Application logs.
- Database activity.
- Customer databases.
- Consent information.
- Existing privacy policies.

These sources provide the information needed for monitoring.

## Step 2: Discover Personal Data

The system identifies personal-data-related information available in the connected sources.

Examples include:

- Email addresses.
- Phone numbers.
- Customer addresses.
- Payment-related information.
- Other personal-data fields.

It uses available log metadata, masking information, database schema information, and data inventories.

## Step 3: Understand Data Flows

The platform maps the movement and usage of personal data across different services.

It helps answer:

- Which service accesses a particular data field?
- Which purpose is associated with that processing?
- Which application or recipient receives the data?
- What is the relationship between services and personal-data fields?

## Step 4: Configure Privacy Policies

Administrators can configure privacy-related rules, including:

- Consent requirements.
- Privacy notice requirements.
- Declared processing purposes.
- Marketing consent.
- Retention periods.
- Erasure requirements.
- Breach detection conditions.

Policies are editable and versioned, with change notes and previews.

## Step 5: Monitor Processing Activities

NigraniAI analyses available system logs and processing events.

It identifies personal-data-related activity and checks whether the activity is consistent with configured policies.

## Step 6: Detect Potential Compliance Issues

The system identifies findings such as:

- Missing consent.
- Processing before consent.
- Processing for undeclared purposes.
- Outdated privacy notices.
- Processing after an erasure request.
- Retention deadlines.
- Suspicious access patterns.
- Unexpected data activity.

## Step 7: Investigate Using AI Assistance

The AI assistant helps administrators understand findings, investigate issues, interpret available evidence, and identify possible fixes and preventive measures.

## Step 8: Support Remediation and Compliance Readiness

The platform provides incident-management workflows, retention worklists, investigation details, readiness scoring, and audit evidence.

The objective is to help organisations move from identifying a privacy issue to understanding and managing it.

The following sections describe the actual modules visible in the compliance monitoring system.

- Logs grouped by service.
- API response breakdown.
- Consent coverage indicators.
- Breach signals by detector.
- Retention status by rule.

The dashboard refreshes its monitoring information periodically and highlights important findings.

### Purpose

To provide administrators with one place to understand the organisation's current monitoring and compliance situation.

## 4.2 Log Telemetry – Application and Database Monitoring

Log Telemetry monitors structured logs generated by different application services.

### What it monitors

- Application events.
- Database events.
- HTTP requests.
- SQL-related activity.
- Service-level activity.
- Errors and warnings.
- Personal-data-related operations.

### Important capabilities

**Service filtering:** View activity from individual services.

**Log filtering:** Filter by time range, severity, application or database layer, event type, personal-data presence, and text.

**Personal data indicators:** Identify which personal-data categories are involved in an event.

Examples:

- EMAIL – Read.
- ADDRESS – Multiple writes.
- CREDIT_CARD – Masked.

**Event details:** View timestamps, service information, trace information, actor, purpose, source information, and available masked log details.

**Live monitoring:** Newly ingested log information is periodically retrieved and displayed.

### Personal data detection approach

The system uses information supplied by the upstream logging and masking process.

Personal-data indicators include masked values and schema classifications. This dashboard module reads those indicators rather than running a separate machine-learning PII model on every log line.

### Purpose

To provide visibility into how applications and databases interact with personal data.

## 4.3 Consent & Purpose – Consent and Processing Validation

This module checks whether processing activity is supported by the relevant consent and declared purpose.

It is one of the central compliance capabilities of NigraniAI.

### What it checks

- Whether a customer accepted the privacy notice.
- Whether processing happened after consent was given.
- Whether marketing processing has the required opt-in consent.
- Whether processing is associated with a declared purpose.
- Whether the accepted notice is outdated.
- Whether processing continues after an erasure request.
- Whether processing is linked to a known customer.
- Whether consent records match the declared data inventory.

### Eight default policy rules

| Rule | Description | Default severity |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Processing without an accepted privacy notice | High |
| R2 | Processing before consent was given | High |
| R3 | Marketing without the required opt-in consent | Critical |
| R4 | Processing for an undeclared purpose | High |
| R5 | Consent associated with an outdated notice | Medium |
| R6 | Processing after an erasure request | Critical |
| R7 | Processing not linked to a known customer | Medium |
| R8 | Consent records differing from the declared inventory | Medium |

### Module functionality

- Consent overview.
- Customer-wise consent status.
- Customer consent timelines.
- Open compliance issues.
- Issue severity.
- Evidence associated with findings.
- Rule configuration.
- Policy version history.
- Purpose and processing-source configuration.
- Data inventory comparison.

Administrators can edit policy rules, preview the impact of changes, add a change note, and save a new policy version.

### Purpose

To compare actual processing activity with the available consent records, notices, and declared purposes.

## 4.4 Data Map – Personal Data Inventory and Flow Mapping

The Data Map provides visibility into where personal data exists and how it moves across organisational services.

### Main components

- Services.
- Personal-data fields.
- Processing purposes.
- Data recipients.
- Data inventory.
- Records of processing activities.

Consider an e-commerce application called ShopSmart.

It may contain the following services:

- Customer Service.
- Authentication Service.
- Order Service.
- Payment Service.
- Delivery Service.
- Marketing Service.

These services may interact with information such as:

- Customer name.
- Email address.
- Phone number.
- Delivery address.
- Order details.
- Payment-related information.

The Data Map connects services, data fields, purposes, and recipients to show the relationships between them.

### Purpose

To help administrators understand where personal data is used, which services access it, and why it is processed.

## 4.5 Retention – Data Retention and Erasure Monitoring

The Retention module evaluates retention schedules against available organisational data.

Different categories of information can have different retention requirements.

### What it monitors

- Records past their retention deadlines.
- Records approaching their deadlines.
- Erasure requests.
- Legal holds.
- Inactive customer accounts.
- Session token expiry.
- Retention-rule conflicts.
- Data requiring review, deletion, or anonymisation.

### Main sections

**Retention Overview**

Displays past-deadline records, upcoming deadlines, open erasure requests, legal holds, and rule-level status.

**Records**

Provides record counts, reasons, relevant rules, and exportable worklists.

**Erasure & Inactivity**

Shows erasure checklists, inactive accounts, and related deadlines.

**Retention Schedule**

Allows administrators to configure retention rules, preview their effects, and maintain policy versions.

### Important implementation behaviour

The current system is read-only with respect to the connected organisational databases.

It does not automatically delete customer records.

Instead, it identifies the records requiring action, checks erasure progress, and generates worklists for the relevant service or administrator.

### Purpose

To help organisations monitor retention obligations and identify records that may require further action.

## 4.6 Breach Response – Potential Breach Detection and Incident Management

The Breach Response module monitors available activity logs for suspicious events and potential personal-data breaches.

It uses configured detectors and a machine-learning anomaly model.

### Detection mechanisms

**Rule-based detectors**

The system contains seven editable detectors that evaluate configured activity conditions.

Administrators can configure:

- Conditions.
- Measurements.
- Thresholds.
- Time windows.
- Grouping.
- Breach type.
- Severity.
- Auto-open behaviour.

**Machine-learning anomaly detection**

The platform uses an Isolation Forest anomaly model.

The model learns normal activity patterns from available logs and highlights unusual behaviour.

For example:

- An unusually high number of database reads.
- Unexpected data-access activity.
- Abnormal service activity.
- Unusual data-egress patterns.

The model provides scores and supporting information to help administrators review unusual events.

### Main sections

**Breach Overview**

Displays open incidents, upcoming deadlines, signals requiring review, and detector activity.

**Signals**

Displays detected signals with severity, event information, reasons, and supporting log information.

Administrators can review, dismiss, reopen, or associate signals with incidents.

**Breach Register**

Maintains incident records and their status.

**Incident Details**

Supports the following incident lifecycle:

Suspected → Confirmed → Contained → Closed

An incident can also be marked as Not a breach.

**Anomaly Model**

Displays model status, activity scores, service baselines, anomaly information, and model settings.

**Detectors & Settings**

Allows administrators to configure detection rules and response settings.

### Response timelines

The system includes configurable response clocks for:

- CERT-In reporting.
- Initial Data Protection Board intimation.
- Customer notifications.
- Detailed Board reporting.

The configured default targets are documented in the existing system and should not be presented as a guarantee of legal compliance.

### Important distinction

A suspicious signal is not automatically a confirmed breach.

Signals must be reviewed and investigated before the incident is classified.

The system prepares notification drafts; it does not automatically send notifications to regulators or customers.

### Purpose

To identify potential breach-related activity and help administrators manage investigation and response workflows.

## 4.7 AI Assistant – AI-Powered Compliance Investigation

NigraniAI includes an AI assistant designed for compliance analysts and administrators.

It answers questions using information available in the compliance dashboard.

### Main capabilities

- Explain compliance issues.
- Investigate findings.
- Suggest corrective actions.
- Recommend preventive measures.
- Explain retention requirements.
- Interpret consent-related findings.
- Help understand breach signals.
- Answer questions about readiness information.

### Suggested interaction categories

- Explain.
- Investigate.
- Fix.
- Prevent.

### Example questions

- What needs my attention most today, and why?
- Explain the consent issues in simple language.
- Why was this activity flagged?
- What does this retention rule require?
- What should I investigate first?
- How can this issue be prevented?

### How the AI assistant works

The browser prepares a minimised snapshot of relevant dashboard information.

The backend adds the system instructions and legal-reference context, then sends the prepared context to the configured AI provider.

The assistant structures responses around:

1. Answer.
2. Why it matters.
3. What to do now.
4. How to prevent it.

The assistant is intended to support human investigation, not replace legal or compliance professionals.

AI-generated answers must be reviewed because they may be inaccurate.

### Purpose

To make compliance information easier to understand and help administrators investigate findings without manually analysing every screen.

## 4.8 Access Management – Staff Permissions and Policy Control

The Access Management module controls what different staff members can do inside the compliance platform.

It follows an AWS IAM-style permission model.

### Main capabilities

- User management.
- Group management.
- Policy management.
- Role and permission assignments.
- JSON-based access policies.
- Allow and Deny decisions.
- Wildcards and conditions.
- Policy validation.
- Policy simulation.

The current system includes a catalogue of 37 actions.

### Policy simulator

Administrators can select a user, specify an action, and simulate whether the request should be allowed or denied.

The platform follows a deny-by-default approach, with explicit deny taking precedence.

### Purpose

To control access to compliance information and administrative functions according to assigned permissions.

## 4.9 DPDP Readiness Score – Compliance Readiness Assessment

This module provides an overall readiness score between 0 and 100.

It evaluates the organisation across eight areas.

### Eight assessment areas

1. Security Monitoring.
2. Breach Readiness.
3. Consent & Purpose.
4. Retention.
5. Access Control.
6. Logging Coverage.
7. Public Exposure.
8. Records & Evidence.

### Score grades

| Score | Grade |
|---|---|
| 85–100 | A |
| 70–84 | B |
| 55–69 | C |
| 40–54 | D |
| Below 40 | E |

The readiness score is calculated using the assessed areas.

Each finding provides relevant information, a regulatory reference, and a suggested fix.

### Public Exposure Test

The system checks what information is accessible through the connected shop database's public key.

This helps identify potentially exposed database tables.

### Evidence & Audit

The platform maintains:

- Activity records.
- Audit information.
- Tamper-evident evidence records.
- Evidence packs.
- Integrity verification.

The evidence ledger uses hash chaining so that modifications to previous records can be detected.

### Purpose

To provide an overview of readiness, highlight areas requiring attention, and maintain supporting audit evidence.

# 6. Technical Architecture – How the Existing System Is Built

The existing compliance monitoring system is implemented using a relatively lightweight architecture.

### Frontend

- HTML.
- CSS.
- JavaScript.
- Inline SVG charts.
- Browser-based module interfaces.

### Backend

- Python standard library.
- A single Python service.
- Authentication verification.
- IAM permission checks.
- Compliance engines.
- Retention calculations.
- Breach detection.
- AI request handling.
- Usage and billing operations.

### Data sources and services

- Supabase Authentication.
- GuardRail Supabase project for staff authentication and activity logs.
- A separate ShopSmart Supabase database containing customer and business information.
- Groq-hosted AI model for the assistant.
- Razorpay for billing and payments.

### Important architecture behaviour

1. The browser uses public configuration values only.
2. Secret keys remain on the server.
3. Staff authentication is handled through Supabase Auth.
4. Backend API requests are verified using the user's JWT.
5. Permissions are evaluated before protected actions.
6. Connected shop data is accessed through the backend.
7. Shop database access is read-only.
8. Personal data is minimised before being returned to the browser or sent to the AI.
9. Policies and operational records are maintained with versioning and audit information.

### Data storage

The current system uses JSON-based server-side storage for configuration and operational records such as:

- Consent policies.
- Retention policies.
- Breach detectors.
- Incident registers.
- IAM policies.
- Usage information.
- Billing information.
- Readiness history.
- Evidence ledger.

The current implementation is not a multi-tenant SaaS platform with one central provider dashboard. Each client receives their own customised copy of the system.

# 7. Important Product Limitations and Accuracy Requirements

The landing page must communicate actual capabilities without exaggerating them.

The following limitations are documented in the existing system:

1. The connected organisational databases are read-only.
2. Retention monitoring creates worklists; it does not automatically delete data.
3. Breach notifications are drafts; they are not automatically sent.
4. A detected anomaly is a signal for review, not proof of a breach.
5. Breach detection depends on available logs and configured detectors.
6. The AI assistant can make mistakes and requires human review.
7. Data Principal rights requests and grievances are not yet implemented.
8. Children's data compliance features are not yet implemented.
9. Significant Data Fiduciary-specific duties are not yet implemented.
10. Some breach situations outside monitored logs must be reported and handled manually.

Do not advertise unimplemented functionality as an existing feature.

Do not claim that NigraniAI guarantees complete DPDPA compliance, prevents every breach, or replaces legal professionals.

Use phrases such as:

- Supports DPDPA compliance monitoring.
- Helps identify potential privacy violations.
- Provides visibility into personal-data processing.
- Helps organisations investigate suspicious activities.
- Supports compliance readiness assessments.
- Helps maintain evidence for reviews and audits.

NigraniAI is a privacy intelligence and compliance monitoring platform that helps organisations understand how personal data is handled across their systems.
